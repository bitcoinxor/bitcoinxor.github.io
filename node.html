Verify it yourself.

During a chain split, explorers disagree, exchanges pick sides, and social media is noise. A node of your own is the only answer to "which chain am I actually on?" that doesn't require trusting anyone. Here's the whole procedure.

What you need

  • Disk: a full node stores the whole chain — roughly 750 GB and growing. With prune=550 that drops to under 10 GB (you keep full validation, you lose the ability to serve history).
  • Machine: anything reasonably modern works; even a small PC or mini-server. Initial sync takes hours-to-days depending on hardware and bandwidth.
  • Software: Bitcoin Knots — the node software of the enforcing chain. Current release: 29.4.knots20260508.

Step 1 — install Bitcoin Knots

Download from bitcoinknots.org only — during a fork event, mirrors and lookalike sites are a real attack vector — and verify the release signatures before running anything. The official site warns, correctly, against downgrading or switching node software based on social-media advice.

Step 2 — configuration for the split

Two settings matter right now. In your bitcoin.conf:

# blocks on the enforcing chain are slow until the PoW change;
# without this a fresh node may think it is still syncing forever
maxtipage=2592000

# optional: prune to ~550 MB of blocks if disk is tight
# prune=550

The maxtipage value is the official guidance from bitcoinknots.org for the current slow-block period.

Step 3 — confirm which chain you're on

Once synced past height 961633, ask your node for that block's hash:

$ bitcoin-cli getblockhash 961633
If you get…You are on…
0000000000000000000139eda19858a84b2ed80cb6056ba8449f0792d13d0968 the enforcing chain (the one changing proof-of-work)
any other hash the majority-hashrate chain

Both chains agree on everything through block 961632 (…c78dbc16) — that block is shared history.

What to expect day to day

  • Slow blocks. Until the proof-of-work change, only a small fraction of hashrate mines this chain — gaps of hours between blocks are normal, not a sign your node is broken.
  • Fewer peers than you're used to. The enforcing-node network is smaller than the pre-split network. Patience during initial sync.
  • Your wallet still works. A node is also the most honest wallet backend there is — see wallet safety before spending anything on either chain.
The proof-of-work change itself The hard fork proposed for 1 September 2026 will require a node release that understands the new proof-of-work. no official fork release exists yet — when it is published, this page gains the upgrade procedure. Until then, be deeply suspicious of any binary claiming to be "the fork node": check bitcoinknots.org and this page rather than links from social media or DMs.

Checking status

Useful commands while you watch the split unfold:

# tip height, hash, and sync state
$ bitcoin-cli getblockchaininfo

# how long since the last block (slow is normal pre-fork)
$ bitcoin-cli getbestblockhash
$ bitcoin-cli getblockheader $(bitcoin-cli getbestblockhash) | grep time

# peer count
$ bitcoin-cli getconnectioncount
